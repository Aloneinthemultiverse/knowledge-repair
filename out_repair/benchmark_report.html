Problem 7 · The AI That Forgot Everything

Knowledge Repair Benchmarks

A repair tool for a corrupted knowledge base of people, places, events and relationships, tested on real Wikidata records with injected errors and on the standard data-cleaning benchmarks from VLDB papers. No labelled data and no AI model are used: every fix comes from evidence inside the data and is logged with its reason.

Cross-record checks

Contradictions found between related records

  • An award event's year disagrees with its own label or with the award links that point to it: resolved by vote.
  • A laureate's age at the award is outside 17–100, or their lifespan is outside 15–110 years: flagged.
  • Death date before birth date: swapped back when the award dates confirm the order.
  • Two copies of a person share a birthday but not a birth year: merged, and the year with a plausible age at the award is kept.
  • A parent link without its inverse child link: restored, because 80%+ of family links in the data have one.
  • A family link implies a gender (father, mother) that conflicts with the record: flagged.

Limits

Where it falls short

  • Flights: 38 websites report times for the same flights and some are wrong more often than right. Without labels, majority voting cannot tell which sources to trust.
  • Rayyan: most errors are dates with their parts reordered. No rule in the data reveals the reordering.
  • Same name, same country: Tokyo the city and Tokyo the prefecture, or two Winchesters in the UK, cannot be told apart from the record alone, so a few places merge wrongly.
  • Wrong country on a duplicated place: a country mismatch blocks the merge on purpose, because it also keeps Cambridge (UK) and Cambridge (US) apart.
  • Errors with no evidence: a typo in a unique name or a missing birth date with no second copy is reported but left as it is.
Method

How the repair works

  • Repairs run in dependency order: places, then events, then people, then relationships, each using the cleaned tables before it as evidence.
  • Duplicates are matched with name normalisation (nicknames, "Last, First", initials), fuzzy similarity and shared birthdays, with country and sibling guards against wrong merges.
  • Typos are fixed only when a rare spelling is one typing error away from a common one.
  • Each change is applied at confidence ≥ 0.90, applied and marked for review at 0.60–0.90, and only flagged below 0.60.
  • Original records are never overwritten. Every repaired entity lists the source record IDs it came from, and every action records its rule and reason.
